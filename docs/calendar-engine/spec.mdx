# Calendar Engine — Specification

One deterministic engine. It reads the option chain, enumerates every calendar
you would consider trading, computes the math on both legs, and ranks them.

Backend only. No UI changes in this phase.

---

## 0. Decisions locked

Confirmed by the trader on 2026-07-27. These are not defaults to be re-litigated.

| Decision | Value |
|---|---|
| Front (short) leg DTE | **floor 15, hard.** Ceiling configurable, default 60 |
| Gap between legs | **floor 15 days, hard.** Ceiling 90 (the data ceiling, §3.5) |
| Working windows in use | 15/30, 21/45, 30/60, 21/60 — all must fall inside the enumeration |
| Wing | **Puts only** |
| Incumbent picker engine | **Replaced.** New engine ships, then the old one is deleted |
| Output | **Ranked full calendars** — each row is one strike + one front expiry + one back expiry |
| Position delta | Delta-neutral at inception |

The floors are hard because they are the trader's rule, not a tuned parameter. The
engine must not be configurable below them. Everything above the floor is a knob.

## 1. The trade, precisely

A long put calendar on SPX: **sell the near expiry, buy the far expiry, same strike.**

Max loss is the debit paid. The structure is delta-neutral, short gamma, long theta,
and — this is the part that matters — **only apparently long vega**.

The doctrine is unanimous that a calendar is not a theta trade:

> "When trading a calendar spread, you are essentially expressing a view on forward
> volatility."
> — [calendar-spread-strategy](https://www.predictingalpha.com/blogs/calendar-spread-strategy)

Everything in §5 follows from taking that sentence literally.

## 2. What the doctrine says

Condensed from the full corpus. The complete dossier with per-article attribution is
[doctrine.md](./doctrine.md); this section is only what the engine encodes.

### 2.1 The one edge claim

Across 103 articles there is exactly one source of edge: the **variance risk premium**,
the tendency of implied volatility to exceed subsequently realized volatility. SPX
anchors: `IV − RV ≈ 4 vol points`, `IV30/RV20 ≈ 1.3`, positive on ~85% of days.

### 2.2 A calendar is a forward-volatility trade

The forward volatility between two expiries is variance additivity:

```
σ_fwd = sqrt( (σ_b²·T_b − σ_f²·T_f) / (T_b − T_f) )
```

Verified against three of the author's own worked examples — his toy case (week 1 at
50%, month at 30% → weeks 2–4 at 19%; formula gives 19.5%), the live KRE trade (11 DTE
at 105%, 39 DTE at 72% → 55%; formula gives 53.8%), and the calendar article (30d 40%,
90d 35% → 32%; formula gives 32.2%). The residual 1–2% is day-count convention.

This identity already exists in the repo as `computeFwdIv`
(`packages/core/src/picker/domain/fwd-iv.ts:30`), with a tagged `{fwdIv: null, guard:
"inverted"}` union instead of a NaN. Reuse it. Do not reimplement it.

### 2.3 The root-time law — and why net vega must never be scored

This is the deepest idea in the corpus and the one prohibition stated outright.

Short-dated IV reacts to a shock more violently than long-dated IV, roughly `∝ 1/√T`.
Vega grows `∝ √T`. The products cancel:

```
vega(T) × Δσ(T) ≈ constant
```

So a calendar is **root-time flat**: a normal, √T-conforming volatility move produces
neither gain nor loss on the vega leg.

> "if normal volatility movements occur across the term structure, we neither lose nor
> gain money due to our vega exposure"
> — [calendar-spread-strategy](https://www.predictingalpha.com/blogs/calendar-spread-strategy)

**Therefore all calendar alpha is the non-root residual** — deviations of the term
structure from the √T pattern. And therefore:

> **Net vega is not a score term.** It is flat by construction. Ranking on it ranks
> nothing. Compute it, display it, never weight it.

The author's own printed shock table is arithmetically inconsistent with his own printed
shock formula, and its 365-day row breaks the invariant that the other four rows
satisfy. Encode the law. Discard the cells.

### 2.4 Raw net theta must not be scored either

Theta is bought with short gamma in exact proportion. A high-theta ranking is a
most-dangerous-front-expiry ranking wearing a friendly label. The author measures theta
as a **percentage of remaining extrinsic value** — 3% per day at 30 DTE, 10% at 10 DTE,
100% on the last day. Normalise or drop it.

### 2.5 The Forward Factor

His named ranking metric for calendars, and the only place he publishes an entry
threshold. He refuses to give the formula:

> "the paper uses a ratio of implied and forward volatility. I use a tool that helps me
> calculate this ratio as a 'Forward Factor'."
> — [kre-is-it-crashing-yet](https://www.predictingalpha.com/blogs/kre-is-it-crashing-yet-calendar-trade-analysis)

Reverse-engineered against his one fully-numbered case (May 19 $36P at 80% IV, June 16
$36P at 64% IV, stated FF = 49%):

```
FF = IV_front / σ_fwd − 1
```

At front ≈14 / back ≈42 DTE this gives 47.4%; at 16/44 it gives 51.7%. The stated 49%
sits inside that window. The two alternative readings — `IV_front/IV_back − 1` = 25%
and `IV_back/σ_fwd − 1` = 18% — do not fit. **This is inference, and it is marked as
inference in code.** Make the expression swappable.

Published gates: **≥ 20%** ("I generally consider trades with a Forward Factor of 20%
to be worth taking"), **≥ 16%** (the terminal's "Best Calendars To Buy" scan), and a
third gate of a different kind — FF above the upper bound of its own trailing 1-year
band.

### 2.6 The cushion

The loss condition, stated directly:

> "My calendar spread would only lose money if implied volatility for the long option
> fell to 55%, a drop of nearly 20 points."
> — [kre-a-case-study-in-forward-volatility](https://www.predictingalpha.com/blogs/kre-a-case-study-in-forward-volatility)

```
cushion = IV_back − σ_fwd        (vol points; KRE had 72 − 55 = 17)
```

And the asymmetry that makes the structure work, credited to Prof. Jim Campasano:
forward volatility is a **biased** forecast of future implied volatility, so the back
leg's IV rarely collapses all the way down to it.

### 2.7 The sharpest warning: an expensive back leg

Easy to miss and it invalidates the whole trade. The author tested a 90-DTE long
strangle as a hedge on a 7-DTE short program and rejected it: the hedge consumed
**~1/3 of profits** and **over 60% of collected premium** while only halving max loss
($4,000 → $2,400 per lot).

**A calendar *is* that hedged structure.** The back leg must be cheap in vol terms or
the calendar converts a positive-EV short into a negative one.

### 2.8 What the doctrine forbids ranking on

| Forbidden | Reason |
|---|---|
| Net vega | Root-time flat (§2.3) |
| Raw net theta | Bought with short gamma in proportion (§2.4) |
| IV rank / IV percentile | "measure IV relative to its historical implied volatility, but it doesn't tell us whether it's too high or too low" |
| Probability of profit | EV is structure-invariant |
| Structure itself | "We don't get paid because of the structure that we trade" |

### 2.9 Where ATM is measured

Define ATM as **delta ≈ 50 per expiry**, not as a shared strike. The 50-delta strike
differs between expiries. Reading two IVs at one shared strike mixes term structure with
skew. §3.3 shows what that costs us in practice.

### 2.10 The exit

One mechanical calendar exit in the whole corpus: **close when the front expiry is 3–5
days away.** Compatible with a 15-DTE entry floor. Out of scope for a ranking engine;
recorded here so it is not lost.

## 3. What our own data says

**This section is the reason the engine cannot simply implement §2.** Every number below
was measured against the production database on cycle `2026-07-27`, puts only, front
15–60 DTE, gap ≥15, back ≤90, both legs carrying a solved `bsm_iv`.

### 3.1 The candidate space is small enough to enumerate exhaustively

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Distinct `(root, front, back)` expiry pairs | **124** |
| Full candidates (strike × pair) | **2,454** |
| Candidates with a solved IV on both legs | 2,454 (100%) |
| Both legs two-sided (`bid > 0`) | 2,454 (100%) |
| Front in 15–30 / 21–45 / 30–60 / 21–60 | 1,883 / 1,482 / 640 / 1,608 |

There is no sampling problem and no need for heuristic pruning. One pass over ~2,500
candidates, deterministic, sub-second. Full enumeration is the design.

### 3.2 The doctrine's absolute gates cannot fire on SPX

This is the single most important finding in this document.

| Doctrine threshold | Its value | Ours, measured |
|---|---|---|
| Forward Factor entry gate | **≥ 16–20%** | **0 of 2,465 candidates.** Max 14.4%, p90 7.1%, **median 0.36%** |
| Cushion `IV_back − σ_fwd` | **17–20 vol points** (KRE) | **median +0.02 points.** p10 −0.49, p90 +0.47 |
| Backwardation at the strike | the condition being screened for | 1,292 / 2,465 (52%) — but see §3.3 |

The reason is scale. His calendars ran on single names and sector ETFs at **60–105% IV**.
SPX runs at **16%**. Dispersion scales with the level, so every absolute vol-point
threshold in the corpus is roughly an order of magnitude too large for this underlying.

> **Consequence: no absolute doctrine threshold may be used as a gate.** An engine that
> gates on FF ≥ 16% returns an empty list every day, forever, and looks like it is
> working.

His own third gate — FF above the upper bound of its trailing 1-year band — is the right
idea and we cannot compute it either (§3.5). The resolution is §6: rank
**cross-sectionally within the snapshot**.

### 3.3 Unadjusted Forward Factor is a skew detector, not a term-structure detector

Ranking the 2,465 candidates by FF computed from each candidate's own two strike IVs:

| Rank | Root | Strike | Front/Back | IV front | IV back | σ_fwd | FF | Distance from spot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SPXW | 7100 | 31/46d | 20.01% | 19.22% | 17.49% | **14.4%** | **302 pts** |
| 2 | SPX | 7300 | 16/53d | 18.17% | 16.67% | 15.98% | 13.7% | 102 pts |
| 3 | SPXW | 7125 | 31/46d | 19.64% | 18.91% | 17.32% | 13.4% | **277 pts** |
| 4 | SPXW | 7150 | 31/46d | 19.31% | 18.61% | 17.09% | 13.0% | **252 pts** |

Best near-ATM candidate (|K − S| ≤ 25): **FF 7.7%** — roughly half.

SPX put skew is steeper in the front expiry than the back, so a deep-OTM strike shows
local backwardation that has nothing to do with the term structure. **An engine that
scores per-strike FF systematically recommends far-OTM puts** — which also violates the
delta-neutral requirement and, per the doctrine, eats negative spot-vol correlation.

> **Consequence: the term-structure score is computed from each cohort's ATM (50Δ) IVs,
> not from the traded strike's IVs.** Per-strike FF is displayed, never scored.

This cleanly separates the two axes, and it matches the teaching already on the
Analyzer's Pair panel: *vertical skew picks the strike; the forward-vol edge picks the
expiry pair.*

### 3.4 The incumbent liquidity gate is wrong for SPX

`isLiquidQuote` (`packages/core/src/picker/domain/rules.ts:103`) requires
`spread/mid ≤ 10%` **and** `openInterest ≥ 100`. Measured over 1,218 SPX puts at 15–90 DTE:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Spread / mid | p10 **0.4%**, p50 **0.6%**, p90 **1.0%**, max 7.8% |
| Open interest | p25 **4**, p50 **39**, p75 153, p90 532 |
| Legs with OI = 0 | 191 |
| Legs with OI < 100 | **829 / 1,218 (68%)** |
| Near-ATM SPXW legs failing OI ≥ 100 | **175 / 255 (69%)** |
| Candidates surviving OI ≥ 100 on both legs | **450 / 2,454 (18%)** |
| Of those 450, additionally failing the spread test | **0** |

Two conclusions.

1. **The spread gate is inert.** The entire distribution sits inside 1% of mid. PA's
   12%/22%/58% figures are for retail single-name weeklies. It cannot discriminate here,
   so a cost or liquidity **score term would be pure noise** — near-constant across every
   candidate.
2. **The OI gate is actively harmful.** It removes 82% of candidates, including 69% of
   the near-ATM SPXW ladder — precisely the strikes the doctrine says to trade. Open
   interest on a fresh SPXW weekly strike is legitimately low; that is not illiquidity.

`volume` is also stored (`schema.ts:141`) and is equally thin at these tenors (near-ATM
median 1). Neither open interest nor volume is a valid liquidity proxy for SPX weeklies.
**The two-sided quote is.**

### 3.5 The history ceiling — no percentile of anything is honest yet

| Source | Range | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| `leg_observations` | 2026-06-12 → 07-27 | **30 distinct days** (24 weekdays), 14.5M rows |
| `gex_snapshots` | 2026-06-23 → 07-27 | 25 days |
| `skew_observations` | 2026-06-18 → 07-27 | 23 days, 610k rows |
| `risk_reversal_observations` | 2026-06-18 → 07-27 | 23 days; 4,819 of 8,327 rows carry a rank |
| `term_structure_observations` | 2026-07-01 → 07-27 | 17 days — **and keyed by `calendar_id`**, so it only covers pairs already held as positions |
| `macro_observations` VIXCLS | 2026-06-30 → 07-23 | **17 rows** |

Every percentile gate in the doctrine — FF 1-year band, slope percentile, IV percentile
65–80, the VIX 80th-percentile kill switch — needs 250+ observations. We have 17 to 30.
A percentile over 20 samples is a random number with a confident label, and the repo has
already shipped that bug once: the second `risk_reversal_observations` row ever written
reported `rrRank = 100` against a single prior value.

One correction to an earlier audit claim: a per-expiry ATM-IV history **is** derivable
today with a `GROUP BY` over `leg_observations` joined to `contracts` — no migration
needed. The binding constraint is depth, not schema. That matters, because it means the
fix is *waiting*, not *building*.

> **Consequence: v1 ships levels and within-snapshot ranks. Zero time-series
> percentiles.** The history accumulates from the tables that already exist; the band
> gates land in v2 when there are 250 days of them.

## 4. The pipeline

Five pure stages. No I/O below the application boundary. Same snapshot in, byte-identical
ranking out.

```
ForReadingChainForPicker  (existing port, existing repo, unchanged)
        │
        ▼
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. cohorts    group rows by (root, expiration)                │
│               → one spot, ATM strike, ATM IV, per-row delta   │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. enumerate  front cohort × back cohort × shared strike      │
│               under the hard gates                            │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. measure    σ_fwd · cushion · FF(atm) · FF(strike) · slope  │
│               · vSkew both legs · greeks both legs            │
│               · net greeks · debit at haircut · spread cost   │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. score      cross-sectional rank of 4 terms → [0,100]       │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. rank       sort, deterministic tie-break, emit top N       │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Compare to the incumbent: 7 orchestrator steps, 15 injected dependencies, a three-system
VIX entry gate, a sizing tier ladder, two brake evaluators, and a persisted snapshot.
The new engine needs **one** read.

## 5. The math, formula by formula

Every quantity the engine emits. `T` is in years on a 365.25 basis, measured to the
settlement instant (§8.3). IVs are decimals; vol points are `×100` at display only.

### 5.1 Per cohort — one `(root, expiration)` group

| Quantity | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `spot` | one value per cohort | Not `rows[0]`. Assert agreement or take the consensus (§8.5) |
| `atmStrike` | `argmin |K − S|`, ties to the lower strike | Existing: `chain-math.ts:113` |
| `atmIv` | IV at `atmStrike` within this `(expiration, contractType, root)` | Existing: `chain-math.ts:160`. **Never substitutes a neighbouring strike** |
| `delta(K)` | `bsmGreeks(S, K, T, σ_K, r, q, "P").delta` | The chain read carries no delta; it is solved per row |
| `atm50Iv` | IV **interpolated** to exactly \|delta\| = 0.50, linear in delta between the tightest bracketing pair | **This is the IV that feeds the term-structure score.** Interpolated and not picked — see §5.1.1. Null when 50Δ is not bracketed, or the bracket exceeds 0.30 in delta space. Never extrapolated |
| `atm50BracketWidth` | delta-space width interpolated across; 0 on an exact hit | So a reader can see how much of `atm50Iv` is measurement and how much is interpolation |
| `extrinsic(K)` | `mark(K) − max(K − S, 0)`, floored at 0 | Denominator for normalised theta. A mid below intrinsic is a quote artifact, not negative time value |

#### 5.1.1 The 50Δ reference is interpolated, not picked

Two live failures forced this, in sequence.

**Picking the nearest strike degenerates on a sparse cohort** — the same neighbour-substitution
error `atmIv` already refuses, one function along. Live SPX cohorts offered these as their
"nearest to 50 delta":

| DTE | Legs | Strike | IV | \|Δ\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 28 | 1 | 6700 | 26.22% | **0.077** |
| 27 | 2 | 6800 | 24.75% | **0.094** |
| 15 | 4 | 7620 | 11.93% | **0.876** |

A back cohort referencing that 11.93% inflated every candidate in its pair to a **44.37%**
forward factor, against a real maximum of 14.4%.

**Bounding that with a tolerance is not enough**, because a tolerance still lets the front and
back references sit at *different* deltas, and skew turns that into a systematic bias:

| Pair | front \|Δ\| gap | back \|Δ\| gap | FF, nearest strike | FF, interpolated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPX 17/53d | 0.070 | 0.001 | 21.35% | **10.91%** |
| SPXW 35/65d | 0.003 | 0.000 | 0.09% | 0.54% |

Half of that 21% was the mismatch — a 0.43Δ front IV compared against a 0.50Δ back IV. Where both
references genuinely sit at 50 delta the two methods agree to within half a point, so
interpolation costs nothing on a dense ladder and removes an artifact on a sparse one.

Same technique and same never-extrapolate policy as `interpolateRiskReversal`, which is this
repo's established precedent for interpolating a smile and refusing when it cannot be done
honestly.

### 5.2 Per candidate — one `(root, strike, frontExp, backExp)`

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| `fwdIv` | `computeFwdIv(T_f, atm50Iv_f, T_b, atm50Iv_b)` — reuse `fwd-iv.ts`, keep the `inverted` guard |
| `cushion` | `atm50Iv_b − fwdIv` |
| `ffAtm` | `atm50Iv_f / fwdIv − 1` — **the scored term-structure signal** |
| `ffStrike` | `iv_f(K) / fwdIvAt(K) − 1` — displayed only (§3.3) |
| `slope` | `(atm50Iv_b − atm50Iv_f) / (T_b − T_f)` — displayed |
| `hSkew` | `iv_f(K) − iv_b(K)` — displayed |
| `vSkewFront` | `iv_f(K) − atmIv_f` |
| `vSkewBack` | `iv_b(K) − atmIv_b` |
| `legGreeks` | `bsmGreeks` per leg, at that leg's own `(r, q)` and settlement-aware `T` |
| `netGreeks` | `back − front` for each of delta, gamma, theta, vega |
| `thetaCarry` | `θ_f / extrinsic_f − θ_b / extrinsic_b` — normalised decay (§2.4) |
| `debit` | `haircutFill(backQuote, "buy") − haircutFill(frontQuote, "sell")` — reuse the 0.66 model |
| `spreadCost` | round-trip width on both legs, as a fraction of `debit` — displayed |
| `frontVrp` | `atm50Iv_f − RV20` |

Sign convention, load-bearing: net = **back − front**, because the calendar is long the
back leg and short the front. Getting it backwards flips every risk number.

### 5.3 Null honesty

A degraded input nulls **its own** field and nothing else. No field ever returns `NaN`,
a fabricated `0`, or a silently-clean substitute. This is the existing rule in
`chain-math.ts` and it carries forward verbatim — an inverted term structure prices no
forward vol, so there is no edge to quote, so `edge` is `null`, not `0`. The incumbent
scorer substitutes `0` there (`scoring.ts:139`); that is a bug the rewrite fixes.

## 6. The score

Four terms. Each is a **cross-sectional percentile within the snapshot**, not an
absolute threshold — forced by §3.2, and it is the same relative-value frame the
doctrine itself uses. Deterministic: the same snapshot always produces the same ranks,
and no history is required.

| Term | Raw metric | Weight | Picks | Why this and not something else |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fwdEdge` | `ffAtm` | **45** | the pair | The author's own named ranking metric, measured on the 50Δ IVs so it reads term structure and not skew (§3.3). The forward-vol view *is* the trade |
| `frontVrp` | `atm50Iv_f − RV20` | **25** | the pair | The house thesis, and the only term that looks outside the chain — therefore the only one not collinear with `fwdEdge` |
| `deltaBalance` | `−\|netΔ\|` | **30** | the strike | The trader's hard constraint. Measured on the live ladder, `\|netΔ\|` is minimised **near the money** (−0.042 at 736 OTM, +0.008 at 16 OTM, +0.098 at 244 ITM), so it selects the strike the doctrine also prescribes |

`score = Σ (weight × percentileWithinSnapshot(term))`, in `[0, 100]`. Two terms are
strike-invariant and pick the expiry pair; one picks the strike inside it. That split is the
engine's shape.

### 6.0 A fourth term was removed after live measurement

`thetaCarry` — `θ_f/extrinsic_f − θ_b/extrinsic_b` — shipped as a fourth term at weight 20 and
put the top-ranked live candidate **721 points out of the money**, scoring it at the 93rd
percentile of that term. The reason is that theta as a share of remaining extrinsic is
**U-shaped in strike, with its minimum at the money**:

| Strike | Distance from spot | θ / extrinsic |
|---|---|---|
| 6660 | 736 OTM | 0.03022 |
| 7380 | 16 OTM | **0.00744** ← minimum |
| 7640 | 244 ITM | 0.02852 |

So ranking it "higher is better" is an instruction to pick the most extreme strike available. The
doctrine uses that ratio to compare **tenors** at the money (3%/day at 30 DTE, 10% at 10 DTE); it
was never a strike discriminator. `netθ/|netΓ|` was tested as a replacement and is worse — it
runs monotonically from 55,070 deep OTM to negative deep ITM.

The weight went to `deltaBalance`, which the same measurement shows is minimised near the money.
`thetaCarry` remains on every candidate as a reported metric and is never scored.

### 6.1 What is computed, shown, and deliberately not scored

| Quantity | Scored? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `cushion` | No | A monotone re-expression of `ffAtm` from the same two IVs. Scoring both double-counts one signal. Displayed, because it *is* the loss condition |
| `slope`, `hSkew`, `ffStrike` | No | Same two IVs again |
| Net vega | **Never** | Root-time flat. The one explicit prohibition in the corpus (§2.3) |
| Net theta, raw **or** normalised by extrinsic | No | Raw theta is bought with short gamma in proportion (§2.4). Normalising by extrinsic does not fix it — measured U-shaped in strike with its minimum at the money, so ranking it picks the most extreme strike (§6.0) |
| Spread cost / liquidity | No | Measured inert: p90 = 1.0% of mid (§3.4). A near-constant term is noise |
| Open interest | No | Measured harmful as a gate, meaningless as a signal (§3.4) |
| IV rank, VRP rank, FF band | No | 17–30 days of history (§3.5). A rank over 20 samples is a random number |
| Probability of profit | No | EV is structure-invariant |
| GEX fit, event adjustment, breakeven-vs-expected-move, debit fit | No | Five of the incumbent's nine terms; the audit found them cohort-wide constants, near-always satisfied post-gate, or weight-0 display |

Nine terms become four. Every dropped term was dropped for a stated, measured reason.

### 6.2 Ties

Rank descending by `score`. Break ties in this order, all deterministic: higher `ffAtm`,
then smaller `|netΔ|`, then lower `debit`, then lexicographic
`(root, frontExpiry, backExpiry, strike)`. Never rely on input order.

## 7. The gates

Hard, small, and each one earns its place.

| # | Gate | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `frontDte ≥ 15` | **hard floor** | Trader's rule. Not configurable below 15 |
| 2 | `backDte − frontDte ≥ 15` | **hard floor** | Trader's rule |
| 3 | `frontDte ≤ frontDteMax` | default **60** | Covers 15/30, 21/45, 30/60, 21/60 |
| 4 | `backDte ≤ 90` | data ceiling | Ingest bound (`BSM_MAX_DTE = 90`, `apps/worker/src/config.ts:25`) |
| 5 | Both legs quotable | `bid > 0`, `ask ≥ bid`, `spread/mid ≤ 0.15` | §3.4. Loose on purpose — catches genuine garbage, discriminates nothing |
| 6 | Both legs priceable | `bsmIv` non-null **and not the string `'NaN'`**, `> 0` | §8.1 — there are three states, not two |
| 7 | Same root both legs | `root_f === root_b` | Mixing AM and PM settlement changes the front leg's expiry-day risk |
| 8 | Forward vol defined | `computeFwdIv(...).guard === "ok"` | An inverted structure is dropped **with a reason**, never scored as 0 |

### 7.1 Gates deliberately absent

- **`openInterest ≥ 100`** — measured harmful (§3.4).
- **The VIX entry gate.** The incumbent runs three threshold systems (4 arm/disarm rungs,
  a continuous penalty band, a 4-tier ladder) over a once-a-day EOD series that cannot
  flap; its own header concedes the three move independently. And the doctrine's kill
  switch is a *percentile* we cannot compute from 17 rows. v1 emits the VIX **level** as
  context and gates on nothing.
- **Event blackout.** The corpus says nothing about SPX macro dates and the repo already
  had to soften its own penalty 0.5 → 0.25 because it saturated. Emit "front spans
  FOMC/CPI" and "back-only spans X" as unweighted row flags; let the human answer the
  doctrine's own question, *why is IV high*.
- **Sizing and Kelly.** The corpus supplies no distribution for a calendar, only "they

## 8. Landmine guards

Each of these is already paid for in production. Each needs a named guard.

### 8.1 `bsm_iv` has three states, not two

`NULL` = never processed · the **string** `'NaN'` = permanent solve failure · a number =
solved. Every read filters both. A widened read that forgets this fabricates numbers.
Also: the BSM drain is bounded at 800 rows / ~11.7 min, newest-first, so older rows can
stay `NULL` indefinitely. Never read a `NULL` IV as "no such contract."

### 8.2 Root scoping is enforced by signature, not by comment

Every cohort function takes `root` as a parameter, so it cannot be called root-blind.
This is already true of `atmIv` and `riskReversalForExpiry` and it stays true. It was
fixed for a reason: a root-blind cohort once measured a back IV of 68.89% against a
front of 24.69% at the same strike.

### 8.3 One time-to-expiry function

The repo currently has **nine** T conventions, three inside the GEX path alone. Mixing
whole-day and settlement-aware `T` is exactly what made theta read visibly low against
ThinkOrSwim; the fix was applied to the engine's greeks only and the browser now repeats
it (`chain-math.ts:200` uses `dte / 365.25`).

The new engine uses **one** exported T function, taking `root` so it can pick AM 09:30 ET
against PM 16:00 ET settlement. Unifying T is the one step in the plan that changes
numbers on screen, so it lands alone, with a TOS comparison.

### 8.4 Carry comes from the same expiry row as the IV

Four carry regimes exist and have never been reconciled: `bsm_*` was solved with a flat
`r` per date and constant `q = 0.013`; `gex_snapshots.implied_carry` is per-expiry;
the GEX profile hardcodes `0.043 / 0.013` in the same row as `implied_carry`; the browser
silently falls back to `{0.045, 0.013}`. Any reprice takes `r` and `q` from the **same
expiry row** it took IV from, or it drifts from the server. A cohort with no carry entry
is marked, not defaulted.

### 8.5 One spot per cohort, passed explicitly

Today `atmIv` measures against a screen-wide `rows[0].underlyingPrice` while `legGreeks`
prices each leg against its own row's spot — and the chain is a vendor union, so
"first row" is whichever vendor landed first.

### 8.6 `dte` is frozen at the observation day

A row observed yesterday whose expiration *was* yesterday has `dte === 0`, survives a
`dte < 0` filter, and renders today as a tradeable 0DTE cohort. Drop on `dte <= 0`, or
recompute against both the observation day and today. Never recompute `dte` downstream.

### 8.7 Open interest is repaired at the read, not the row

Schwab returns `openInterest: 0` for every contract outside RTH and writes about a minute
after CBOE, so newest-row-wins zeroed ~2,971 contracts a day. The existing read uses
`MAX(open_interest) OVER (PARTITION BY contract)` over the 10-minute union window. Keep
it, even though the engine no longer gates on OI — it still displays it.

### 8.8 Every statistic reports its sample count

No rank, mean, or percentile is emitted without `n`. Below a configured floor the value
is `null`, not a confident number. This is the `rrRank = 100`-against-one-prior-value bug.

## 9. The blocker that comes first

**This is not optional and it is not part of the engine.**

Ingest still writes `root: chain.root` (`packages/core/src/journal/application/fetchChain.ts:143`)
— the *requested chain label*, not the OCC symbol — and formats `expiration` with local
date getters off a UTC-midnight date (`:135-138`). Verified open at HEAD `6622dec`.

Result, measured on production: **1,190 contracts carry `root='SPX'` on an OCC symbol
reading SPXW, and every one also has `expiration` exactly one day early.** `root_only`
mismatches are 0, which proves a single writer.

The corruption is not cosmetic. Those two columns feed `computeT`, which picks AM 09:30 ET
against PM 16:00 ET off `root`. Those legs were solved with `T` short by a full day — plus
6.5 hours where the wrong date lands on a third Friday — so their `bsm_iv` and every greek
are **biased high**, and that flows into `skew_observations`, the risk reversals, GEX gamma
and the chain table.

No engine-side care can detect this. A signature can guard a code path; it cannot guard a
mislabelled row.

Fix order:

1. Derive both `root` and `expiration` from `parseOccSymbol(quote.occSymbol)`. The
   trustworthy values are already computed at `leg-observations.ts:193` and thrown away at
   `:197-207`.
2. Take `upsertContracts` off `onConflictDoNothing()` (`leg-observations.ts:125`) so a
   backfill can land.
3. Backfill `contracts`, then re-solve the affected `leg_observations`.
4. Add an assertion that `contracts.root` equals the OCC root.

Note that commit `555e748` ("carry OCC root so SPX and SPXW stop colliding") fixed the
**read and display** path, not the writer. Easy to mistake for closed.
